## The labour market three ways

The rule's gap is built from LFS unemployment. The claimant count and PAYE payrolled employees are read beside it, as the ONS recommends, and never enter the rule. Each month is as first printed.

- The LFS and the claimant count point different ways over twelve months in 34% of months overall, 46% since 2023. Correlation of the two 12-month changes: +0.72 to 2022, -0.16 since.
- The LFS says unemployment rose while payrolls rose too (or the reverse) in 24% of months since 2020 (PAYE vintages start in December 2019), 36% since 2023.

Not all disagreement is LFS noise. In 2017-19 the claimant count rose while LFS unemployment fell: that is the Universal Credit rollout, which widened who counts as a claimant, so the claimant count has a break of its own there. The disagreement since 2023 has no such explanation on the claimant side.

This is a finding, not a gap to reconcile: the UK labour market input is less reliable than the US one, so the UK signal is noisier, and that should be known before anything is sized on it.

## Data quality, from the config's tags

- **D7G7**: CPI release days before February 2016 are dated the 26th of the month (up to ~10 days late, never early)
- **MGSX**: LFS release days before April 2016 are dated the 26th of the month (up to ~10 days late, never early)
- **MGSX**: LFS unemployment is noisy after the 2023 response-rate collapse: suspended Oct 2023-Jan 2024 (the last official vintage is held), "official statistics in development" Feb 2024-Aug 2026, volatile in mid-2023 and 2024, and a collection error in May-June 2026 degraded releases from July
- **CLAIMANTS**: claimant count is still 'official statistics in development'
- **PAYE**: PAYE RTI vintages exist only from December 2019; not used before then
- **natural_rate**: u* is a constant 4.5%, not a real-time estimate
- **rstar**: r* is a constant (-1.6%, the 2009-26 average real Bank Rate): a hindsight number that moves the bp gap, not the z
