## Credit: does the policy path say anything about spreads?

**The claim.** A credit desk asking whether the policy-path gap belongs in its process gets three answers. First, the path explains almost none of the week-to-week movement in the quality spread. Second, the gap's lead on the spread a quarter out has the sign registered before the test ran: a market that underprices tightening against the rule has come before a wider spread. But it clears the registered line only just, and once the overlap between quarterly changes is priced in it sits at the edge of chance: with the link removed, a t that negative turns up in {credit.USD.headline.t2_placebo_p_pct}% of placebo samples. Nor does it survive the obvious sensitivities. Third, the sign does not demonstrably flip between early and late hiking, and the history is too short to reliably detect a flip smaller than {credit.USD.headline.t3_mde}bp of spread per bp of gap. The gap is a rates signal. It explains too little of the weekly quality spread to hedge it (the option-adjusted spreads, on FRED only since {credit.USD.headline.ig_oas_first_print}, show more: {credit.USD.headline.t1_ig_oas_r2_market_pct}%), and it gives no lead worth trading.

**The spread.** The primary series is Moody's {credit.USD.headline.primary_label}. Both legs are yields on seasoned long-dated corporate bonds, so the maturity and the Treasury benchmark cancel, leaving the price of the lower grade. Baa less the thirty-year and less the ten-year Treasury sit alongside it, but they also carry the curve's own moves; ICE BofA's investment-grade and high-yield option-adjusted spreads are a short cross-check. Spread changes stand in for excess returns: a spread that widens on a bond of duration D loses about D times the widening against Treasuries, with carry and default losses left out. Tests 1 and 3 use weekly changes, the last print on or before each {credit.USD.headline.week_ends}: {credit.USD.headline.weeks} weeks from {credit.USD.headline.first_week} to {credit.USD.headline.last_week}, of which test 1 uses the {credit.USD.headline.t1_quality_weeks} from {credit.USD.headline.t1_quality_first} where every part of the signal exists at both ends of the week, and test 3 the {credit.USD.headline.t3_weeks} off the floor. Test 2 uses the signal's {credit.USD.headline.sessions} sessions with a z, from {credit.USD.headline.first_session}.

Seasoned-bond quotes can trail the market, and a stale spread catching up with last week's Treasury move would look like a lead, so a rule for it was fixed before the predictive test ran. Staleness {credit.USD.headline.stale_primary} in {credit.USD.headline.primary_label}: its weekly changes have a first-order autocorrelation of {credit.USD.headline.stale_quality_ar1} and a correlation of {credit.USD.headline.stale_quality_lag1} with the previous week's change in the ten-year yield, both inside ±{credit.USD.headline.stale_band}. Baa less a Treasury yield trips the rule on both counts (autocorrelations {credit.USD.headline.stale_baa_30y_ar1} for the thirty-year and {credit.USD.headline.stale_baa_10y_ar1} for the ten-year; correlations {credit.USD.headline.stale_baa_30y_lag1} and {credit.USD.headline.stale_baa_10y_lag1}), so as registered those fits carry the ten-year's lagged move as a control. But a quote catching up would give a negative autocorrelation and a positive correlation, and these have the opposite signs. The ten-year spread's autocorrelation is mostly the 2020 crash ({credit.USD.headline.stale_baa_10y_ar1_ex_crash_2020} without it); the thirty-year's is not ({credit.USD.headline.stale_baa_30y_ar1_ex_crash_2020}).

**Test 1: is credit's weekly variation a repricing of the path?** The weekly change in the spread is regressed on the parts of the policy signal: the market's rate for meeting {credit.USD.headline.k} as it stood at the start of the week (held, so that a meeting passing is not read as a repricing), minus the rule's rate for that meeting, the slope orthogonal to the level, and the cross-country differential. The market's part alone explains {credit.USD.headline.t1_quality_r2_market_pct}% of the weekly variance of Baa - Aaa. Every part together explains {credit.USD.headline.t1_quality_r2_components_pct}%, and {credit.USD.headline.t1_quality_r2_control_pct}% with the Treasury curve from ten to thirty years added. Without the crash window ({credit.USD.headline.exclude_first} to {credit.USD.headline.exclude_last}) the figure is {credit.USD.headline.t1_quality_r2_ex_crash_2020_pct}%. Where a spread does seem to move with the path, the benchmark is doing the moving. Baa less the ten-year reads {credit.USD.headline.t1_baa_10y_r2_market_pct}%, most of it the ten-year's own move inside a spread that subtracts it. Over its short sample, ICE investment-grade OAS reads {credit.USD.headline.t1_ig_oas_r2_market_pct}% ({credit.USD.headline.t1_ig_oas_market_coef}bp per bp, t {credit.USD.headline.t1_ig_oas_market_t}): hawkish repricings came with tighter OAS in those years. OAS is also measured against Treasuries, so part of that may be the same benchmark effect.

**Test 2: does the gap lead the spread?** The intuition is that a market underpricing tightening against the rule is probably underpricing its growth consequences too, and that rates adjust before spreads. Three things were written down before the test ran: the sign (a negative slope: a gap below zero predicts wider spreads), one headline cell (Baa - Aaa, the z of the gap at meeting {credit.USD.headline.k}, {credit.USD.headline.h_long} sessions ahead) and the rule that calls it. The slope is {credit.USD.headline.t2_slope}bp of spread per unit of z (Newey-West t {credit.USD.headline.t2_t}). Fitted on non-overlapping quarters instead, the mean slope over every start date is {credit.USD.headline.t2_nonoverlap_mean}, with {credit.USD.headline.t2_nonoverlap_negative_pct}% of them negative and a mean t of {credit.USD.headline.t2_nonoverlap_t}. The headline {credit.USD.headline.t2_verdict} the rule. The rule's second condition turned out weak: the non-overlapping mean it asks to be negative is nearly the pooled slope, and only {credit.USD.headline.t2_nonoverlap_clearing} of the {credit.USD.headline.t2_nonoverlap_offsets} start dates clear the line on their own.

The size matters more than the pass. A quarter's change in Baa - Aaa has a standard deviation of {credit.USD.headline.t2_change_sd}bp, and the gap explains {credit.USD.headline.t2_r2_pct}% of it. The pass rests on a t just past the line, and the standard errors decide it. Quarterly changes a session apart share all but a day, and the registered Newey-West weights give that overlap about two thirds of its weight. With equal weights (Hansen-Hodrick) the t is {credit.USD.headline.t2_uniform_t}, and with twice the lag {credit.USD.headline.t2_bartlett_2h_t}. A placebo added after the run puts a number on it: rotating the spread's daily changes in time against the gap keeps each series' own persistence and removes any link between them. Over {credit.USD.headline.t2_placebo_shifts} rotations the registered t reaches ±1.96 {credit.USD.headline.t2_placebo_reject_pct}% of the time, where it should 5%, and is as negative as the headline's {credit.USD.headline.t2_placebo_p_pct}% of the time.

{credit.USD.headline.t2_cells_negative} of the {credit.USD.headline.t2_cells} registered sensitivities on Baa - Aaa have the registered sign; those that clear the line: {credit.USD.headline.t2_cells_clearing}. None of the registered cells that change the sample or the horizon clears it: without the crash window the slope is {credit.USD.headline.t2_quality_h63_z_ex_crash_2020_slope} (t {credit.USD.headline.t2_quality_h63_z_ex_crash_2020_t}), without the sessions where rate and rule sat on the floor {credit.USD.headline.t2_quality_h63_z_ex_elb_slope} (t {credit.USD.headline.t2_quality_h63_z_ex_elb_t}), and a month ahead {credit.USD.headline.t2_quality_h21_z_all_slope} (t {credit.USD.headline.t2_quality_h21_z_all_t}). Two checks added after the run change the sample too. Without each calendar year in turn, the slope is negative in {credit.USD.headline.t2_years_negative} of the {credit.USD.headline.t2_years} refits ({credit.USD.headline.t2_years_slope_min} to {credit.USD.headline.t2_years_slope_max}); the rule still passes in {credit.USD.headline.t2_years_passing} and fails in {credit.USD.headline.t2_years_failing}, most of all without {credit.USD.headline.t2_without_year1} (t {credit.USD.headline.t2_without_year1_t}) and {credit.USD.headline.t2_without_year2} (t {credit.USD.headline.t2_without_year2_t}). The 2014 z's are inflated by a trailing standard deviation at its floor, but clipping z at ±{credit.USD.headline.t2_z_clip} gives {credit.USD.headline.t2_z_clipped_slope} (t {credit.USD.headline.t2_z_clipped_t}) and leaving out the {credit.USD.headline.t2_floor_sessions} sessions at the floor {credit.USD.headline.t2_above_floor_slope} (t {credit.USD.headline.t2_above_floor_t}): {credit.USD.headline.t2_z_checks_words}. The 2014-15 widening also coincides with the oil collapse and the energy-led credit sell-off, which has nothing to do with the policy path. Baa less the thirty-year and less the ten-year Treasury show larger slopes, {credit.USD.headline.t2_baa_30y_h63_z_all_slope} (t {credit.USD.headline.t2_baa_30y_h63_z_all_t}) and {credit.USD.headline.t2_baa_10y_h63_z_all_slope} (t {credit.USD.headline.t2_baa_10y_h63_z_all_t}), but they carry the Treasury curve's moves as well as credit's. ICE's investment-grade OAS, over its short sample, has {credit.USD.headline.t2_ig_oas_h63_z_all_sign}: {credit.USD.headline.t2_ig_oas_h63_z_all_slope} (t {credit.USD.headline.t2_ig_oas_h63_z_all_t}). The honest reading is weak evidence in the registered direction, at the edge of chance. It is not a lead to trade.

**Test 3: does the sign flip with the cycle?** Early in a hiking cycle a hawkish repricing should read as growth confirmation and tighten spreads; late in one it should read as policy-error risk and widen them. The regimes are real-time labels from the policy rate: early hiking is a cycle's first year, late hiking after that while a hike has come in the last six months, and weeks on the floor are left out. Pooled over every week, the late-minus-early difference in the slope on the level's weekly change is {credit.USD.headline.t3_diff} (t {credit.USD.headline.t3_diff_t}), against a smallest difference the sample could detect with reasonable power of {credit.USD.headline.t3_mde}. By episode:

- {credit.USD.headline.t3_cycle1_early_label} (early): {credit.USD.headline.t3_cycle1_early_slope} (t {credit.USD.headline.t3_cycle1_early_t})
- {credit.USD.headline.t3_cycle1_late_label} (late): {credit.USD.headline.t3_cycle1_late_slope} (t {credit.USD.headline.t3_cycle1_late_t})
- {credit.USD.headline.t3_cycle2_early_label} (early): {credit.USD.headline.t3_cycle2_early_slope} (t {credit.USD.headline.t3_cycle2_early_t})
- {credit.USD.headline.t3_cycle2_late_label} (late): {credit.USD.headline.t3_cycle2_late_slope} (t {credit.USD.headline.t3_cycle2_late_t})

{credit.USD.headline.t3_sentence} The early slopes are {credit.USD.headline.t3_early_signs}, the late slopes {credit.USD.headline.t3_late_signs}. The one late phase with the hypothesised sign is one of {credit.USD.headline.t3_episodes} episode slopes, and its t is short of the line. With this few cycles the power is too low for a test at nominal size, so the claim stops at the sentence above.

**What would change the view.** A third hiking cycle whose late phase again shows a positive slope. The predictive headline holding up as the history grows: without the crash, under the other standard errors and against the placebo. An option-adjusted series long enough to run the tests beyond seasoned Moody's bonds. The predictive result should be dropped if another year of data takes its headline t back inside the line.

**Limitations.** Spread changes are a proxy for excess returns. Moody's yields are on seasoned bonds, and the weekly changes of Baa less the thirty-year Treasury are serially correlated, crash or no crash, for a reason the staleness check does not identify. The regimes come from two round thresholds, and with two hiking cycles no threshold choice gives a test with power. The predictive headline includes the sessions on the floor, which the book's own statistics leave out; without them it does not pass. And its registered standard errors are too small for overlapping quarterly changes: with no link at all, its t clears ±1.96 {credit.USD.headline.t2_placebo_reject_pct}% of the time.
